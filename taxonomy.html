Basis and scope

How to read this taxonomy.

MainlineA broad production domain or approach.
Variants / SubcategoriesCrops, species, system types or methods.
Examples in IndiaConcrete practice illustrations.
  • Proposed framework. The EQIQAI AgroTech taxonomy of Indian farming systems is a proposal. It is not an official Government of India classification.
  • Eight master categories covering crop production, livestock, aquaculture, agroforestry, integrated systems, controlled environments, specialized biology, and cross-cutting agroecological and regenerative approaches.
  • Overlap is by design. For example, regenerative agriculture is a management approach applied within crop, livestock, agroforestry or integrated systems, rather than a separate commodity class.
  • Aligned with KRISHYAM. The structure follows the KRISHYAM “INDIAN AGRICULTURE” eight-box taxonomy diagram.

Cropping variants within crop production

Monocropping
one crop species on a field in a season
Mixed cropping
more than one crop grown without distinct row patterns
Intercropping
spatially arranged mixtures (e.g., paired rows)
Relay cropping
sowing a following crop into a standing crop before harvest
Sequential cropping
back-to-back crops in the same field within a year
Crop rotation
planned sequence over years (e.g., rice-wheat-pulses)
Strip cropping
strips of crops to manage erosion or pests
Multi-storey cropping
crops at different heights in the same area
Perennial cropping
long-lived crops (orchards, plantations)

Cultivation vs allied activity

Aquaculture (freshwater, brackish, marine, integrated) is on-farm cultivation with managed stocking, feeding, breeding and water quality.

Capture fisheries are an allied activity based on wild stocks, governed by stock management and regulations, not farm-level cultivation.

Downstream value chains may be shared, but the taxonomy tags them differently at the production stage.

Important distinction

Controlled environments are not inherently regenerative. Sustainability depends on energy source, water use and recirculation, nutrient regime (synthetic vs organic), and the lifecycle of substrates and plastics.

A hydroponic or vertical farm may be high-efficiency but not regenerative. Approach classification is handled through the Category 8 tags.

Agroecological & Regenerative Approaches

ApproachVariants / SubcategoriesKey emphasis
Regenerative agricultureSoil-first, diversified, agroforestrySoil restoration, ecosystem function, resilience

Natural farmingLocal natural farming, community-managedLocal biological inputs, ecological processes

Important distinction

These approaches are not synonyms. They differ in objectives, standards and certification, permitted practices, and measurement focus.

A farm may adopt several simultaneously, for example conservation tillage + IPM + precision nutrient application + climate-smart planning.

Distinguishing the major management approaches

ApproachPrimary ObjectiveStandards / Certification
OrganicExclude/restrict synthetic inputsThird-party or PGS certification
Natural farmingWork with nature, minimal external inputsOften local/community norms; formal standards vary
Regenerative agricultureRestore soil and ecosystem functionNo single universal standard; outcome-focused
Conservation agricultureReduce erosion and soil disturbanceReference to the three key principles
Climate-smart agricultureProductivity + adaptation + mitigationFramework- and project-based indicators
Precision agricultureOptimize inputs using data and automationOperational best practices, solution-driven
AgroecologyEcological principles with social justice focusParticipatory, movement/research-based

Measurement focus also varies: input compliance, soil organic matter and biodiversity, tillage intensity and residue cover, GHG emissions and sequestration, yield stability, input-use efficiency, and landscape biodiversity and equity.

How to use the taxonomy

Tagging guidance and example tag paths

Categories 1 to 7 tag what is produced. Category 8 tags how it is produced.

Production vs Approach

Principle 1

Sections 1-7 tag what is produced (crops, animals, systems); Section 8 tags how it is produced (management/approach).

Multiple tags allowed

Principle 2

One primary production mainline, optional secondary mainlines (for integrated systems), and one or more approach tags.

Capture fisheries

Principle 3

Tag under Aquaculture & Fisheries; mark as an allied production activity, NOT cultivation.

Example tag paths

Example 1

Conventional rice

Crop Production → Cereal farming → Rice (paddy); no special approach tag.

Example 2

Organic apple orchard

Crop Production → Fruit farming → Temperate → Apple; approach: Organic farming (certified/PAS).

Example 3

Regenerative mango agroforestry with goats

Production: Agroforestry → Agrihorticulture; Livestock → Goat farming; Approaches: Regenerative agriculture, Agroecology, Conservation agriculture.

Example 4

Dairy IFS with biogas

Production: Livestock → Dairy; IFS → Crop-livestock-biogas; Approach: Climate-smart (+ Regenerative).

Example 5

Brackish shrimp farm

Aquaculture → Brackish-water aquaculture → Shrimp farming; Approach: Precision agriculture / Climate-smart where applicable.

Example 6

Coastal capture fishery

Aquaculture → Capture fisheries → Coastal fisheries; Flag: Allied activity; Approach: Agroecology at seascape scale if applicable.

Example 7

Protected hydroponics

CE Farming → Protected cultivation + Hydroponics; Approach: Precision agriculture; optionally Climate-smart.
